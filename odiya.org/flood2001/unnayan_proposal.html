1. Background

 

Triple tragedies that struck Orissa in a short span of eighteen months have left little with the poor of this State. Cyclone and floods, which hit coastal districts during October 1999 and the second and third weeks of July 2001 respectively, were unprecedented in their magnitude. Destruction caused by these two calamities has shattered the myth of ‘coastal well-being’. Standing kharif crops were ravaged in super-cyclone of 1999 posing severe food-insecurity to the people. Subsequently, prospects of rabi crop of the expectant farmers were destroyed due to the drought earlier this year. Now, floods, besides damaging whatever remained of the rabi crop after the drought, have ruled out chances of the Kharif crop in the current season. Probably, anything worse could not have happened to the agrarian economy of the State. 

 

Though details of the casualty and damages due to the flood are not clear yet, according to media reports, seventy lakh people of twenty-one districts have been affected. So far, 45 persons have lost their lives. Hundred of cattle have also perished. A large number of dwelling units have been damaged and marooned people were/are being forced to spend days cramped over embankments and other elevated structures.

 

As flood has worsened already precarious food-insecurity position of among the affected, immediate need in the affected areas is of providing of food. Threat of an epidemic looms large, and measures to prevent it also need to be taken urgently. In the long term, people may need rehabilitation, intensive agricultural extensive services and sustained support through food-for-work initiatives. Considering the urgency to provide immediate relief specifically focuses on provision of food, medicines, shelter and fodder to affected households in Unnayan’s existing project area, which is also one of the worst hit areas. In the second phase, in a fortnight’s time, intervention for rehabilitation will be planned.

3. Situation analysis

 

  1. Gheribandh (embankment) in Thailo gram panchayat, constructed after super cyclone in 1999, has been damaged and needs to be repaired. Agricultural lands in the villages have been flooded, destroying the crop. However, damages to the houses have been limited in the gram panchayat.

 

  1. More than 50% of the houses in villages in Bangurigaon gram panchayat have been damaged. Road communication has been disrupted and people had to spend days cramped at various elevated places in the area. Kharif crop destroyed.

 

  1. Most of the houses in the villages of Jalpok and Pokhat gram panchayats have caved in, causing untold miseries to the people. Crops damaged beyond recovery.

 

  1. In all the places outbreak of epidemic is imminent. Reports of water borne diseases have started coming in. So far, diarhhoea, malaria, respiratory infection, skin diseases, jaundice, snakebites and measles have been reported from these areas. In short term, people need food, medicine, baby food and fodder. Subsequently they also require seeds and other agricultural inputs like fertilizers.

 

4. Objective

 

“To solve problems of food-insecurity, lack of shelter and health in the proposed villages, in the short run.”    

 

5. Project description

 

Following activities will be taken up under the project.

 

A. Distribution of medicines

 

Essential medicines will be distributed to the affected population. Important medicines have been identified as follows.

 

a)     Halogen tablets

b)     Bleaching powder

c)      ORS

d)     Paracetamol

e)     Nimsulide

f)       Antibiotic cream

 

B. Distribution of food-stuff

 

Following items will been distributed among affected population:

 

a) Flattened rice             10 quintals

b) Biscuits

c) Milk powder

d) Rice                            90 quintals

e) Jaggery                      

f) Pulses

g) Fodder (one truck load of nara and one truck load of kunda)

 

C. Distribution of polythene

 

Two thousand polythene sheets, one to each of the household, are proposed to be provided in the villages. This will provide people with temporary shelter. Households would be priortised as per their need for the purpose of distribution.
