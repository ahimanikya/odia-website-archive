Orissa Floods 2001

An Overview:  While Orissa is yet to recover from the shock of the unprecedented natural disaster, the Super Cyclone of October 99, followed by the widest spread famine subsequently in 2000; the State is now face to face with another natural calamity of vast magnitude, that is, the floods of July 2001. Compared with the three major floods in the post-independence period, i.e, in 1955, 1982 & 1994, the current flood of July 2001 is a unique one for its ever-widest spread, the longest duration and extensiveness of damage and devastation. The current flood is also unique for the heavy rains that accompanied it intermittently throughout from its humble start in the first week of July. Though receding on the whole, the floodwaters are still visible everywhere leaving the people hopeless & dump-founded, as it was witenessed in the immediate aftermath of the Super Cyclone. Though the number of human and cattle casualities in the current floods is relatively less than the corresponding figures of earlier floods, the state of their survial has worsened miserably beyond description.   

        The current floods have affected most of the 30 districts of Orissa. It has particularly hit hardest the life and environment of the costal districts, namely Ganjam, Puri, Khurda, Cuttack, Jagatsinghpur, Kendrapara, Jajpur, Bhadrak & Balasore owing to high floods in all major river systems, Mahanadi, Brahmani, Baitarani, Subarnarekha, Budhabalanga & Rushikulya & their tributaries, all falling in Bay Of Bengal. These very districts were also subjected to immeasurable devastation in the Super Cyclone about 20 months back. In quite many places of these districts, the villages remained marooned for more than a week, entirely cut off from outside world, with villagers taking shelter on the roofs of pucca buildings or hilltops under open sky in the drenching rains. The people who could some how escape the surging flood waters, came to the nearest road and took shelter in the make-shift tents made of torn.Polythene sheets that they had some how stored from the Super Cyclone days. Human beings along with their cattle and other domestic animals inhabit each such tent. Call it religious faith or sheer superstition, the villagers who ran away from their homes in the face of the rising flood waves, didn’t however miss to bring along their cattle with them, lest they would sin against the God.

Damage to the life and environment: The damage caused by the ongoing floods is difficult to assess at the present stage. However the following kinds of damage to the immediate life situation of the people are already too apparent : 

Collapse of Kutcha houses rendering thousands and thousands of people homeless and driven to the make-shift tents on

the nearest roads;

Vast stretches of agricultural land with standing crops and vegetables have been badly damaged, beyond recuperation.

The Rural people are now simply perplexed as to what they shall feed on in coming six months or so.            

    C. With the collapse of the sheds for cattle, goats, sheep, & other domestic animals, and inundation of the grasslands the animal population of the village is passing through severe hardships. Most of these animals are still stuck up in the tents on the road-side along with human beings, while others have turned into stray animals.

    D. Since the flood waters have over-run the dug-wells, tube-wells, ponds, canals & rivers, there is dearth of safe water even for bathing purposes, let alone for drinking or cooking by human beings. Compelled to use the dirtied water for drinking, bathing & cooking purpose, the people have already started suffering from different diseases. There is now very much likelihood of out-break of epidemics like diarrhoea, cholera, dysentery & the like. Lack of proper food & nutrition is likely to further aggravate the morbidity scenario in flood-affected villages. 

    E. With the grain stores of the people having been destroyed along with the houses, the affected households have nothing in their possession in the form of staple crops to sustain them in coming six months or so. If there are no alternative programmes for enabling the villagers to arrange for their foodstuff on day-to-day basis by well-planned efforts of both Government and non-Government organizations, it is very much likely that mass starvation shall follow soon, as it happens in case of every natural calamity of extensive magnitude. 

       F. The condition of woman & children & aged persons in the flood-affected villages is worst of all. Especially is miserable the case of pregnant and lactating mothers and their new-borns and kids, who are now deprived of regular supply of milk and essential nutrition. If special & concerted attention is not paid to this critical segment of flood-affected population, both mortality and morbidity of females and infants are likely to take place on a large scale.

       G. The affected households have lost the wherewithals of their subsistence, such as a farmer’s loss of plough and other agricultural implements, a fisherman’s loss of boat and net, an artisan’s loss of his working tools and the like. As a result, even if they want to resume their vocations, they cannot, if no alternative arrangement for provision of lost wherewithals is made. 

Need for Long-term Intervention: The above-mentioned problems, if not addressed to adequately and properly would affect the very day-to-day life of the people in the flood affected villages in the immediate run. However the recent floods devastated not only the natural topography of the affected areas, but also their developmental infrastructure, which require long-term reconstruction and restoration programmes. The following are instances of the kind of damages, that deserve long-term interventions,                   

In quite many places the roads, the river embankments, & canal Bandhs & bridges have been breached resulting in cut

-off in the communication by road. Moreover the season for floods and cyclones not being over yet (mind it, the Super Cyclone of 1999 took place on 29 and 30 October), there is very much likelihood that the breaches so created would work as gate-ways for flood waters & saline waves to spread everywhere quickly without any resistence whatsoever, in the event of occurrence of a flood or cyclone again in this very season itself,

Both electric & telephone systems have been paralysed in the flood affected areas due to serious damage to the poles,

transformers and cables, which if not restored shortly on emergency footing, the day-to-day life of the people would remain paralysed too,

The agricultural fields which have been overrun by flood waters with residues of the receding flood waters accumulated

over them, if not renovated properly can not be used as crop or vegetable fields further,

The common property resources like Panchayat Buildings, Post Offices, Schools, Club houses & play fields etc have

been damaged in the floods.

The infrastructural facilities like worksheds, garages, shops & market places have been damaged in the recent floods,

which if not restored timely, would continue to adversely affect the return to normalcy in affected areas.

Now in the light of the above scenario of damage & devastation caused by the recent floods, it is easy to realise what sort of short-term relief & rehabilitation measures & long-term reconstruction and restoration programmes need be undertaken for bringing back the life to a state of normalcy.

A blessing in disguise ?

The Super Cyclone of 1999 despite its devastation on an unprecedented scale, did play a salutary & positive role for the people in coastal Orissa in the ultimate run. As a  permanent safe-guard against the natural disasters, some affected people in most places went for changing their Kutcha & thatched houses into pucca buildings. A large network of Cyclone shelter buildings with multi-purpose facilities were constructed. Plantations on large-scale sprung up in different places to serve as barrier against the surging saline waves. And so on and so forth. Similarly the current flood could serve as a blessing in disguise if it can open up the eyes of the common people, legislators, NGOs & intelligentsia to plan out anew the flood prevention measures in particular & disaster preparedness & management in general, which in combination with the cyclone prevention & mitigation measures would enable the people & State of Orissa to meet the natural disaster of any kind in future.
