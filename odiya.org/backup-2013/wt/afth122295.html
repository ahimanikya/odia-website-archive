The article was really good. I was particularly captivated by the
lyrical and rhythmic symphony of the poem.

But this I am writing as a strong critic of Mr. Sushanta Mohanty's
observation on Buddhisim. I think he is one of the reviewers.
The topic of Buddhisim and such other subtleties should be treated 
very carefully. 

The concept of "Nirvana" may not come anywhere near what we normally 
mean by "Absolute", not even by any philosophical speculation.

I am cutting and pasting the comments that Sushanta made in his review
below:

" For people like Budha it may be absolute - NIRVANA."

 I think I am not wrong on what I claimed in my letter to you, if my
 understanding of English is not at doubt.

 This exactly what I abhor most among our young generation. Without
 knowing a subject properly they would pass some comments or argue
 for hours.

 Is not that true what I said to you about the above quoated line of 
 Sushanta?

 In simple term "Nirvana" is a method or way preached by Buddha to 
 escape  "Absolute" or "Infinity" which he was realising with
 pain incomprehensible. I am just simplifying it, but it is not as
 simple as one would think, and I don't claim either that I understood
 Buddhism to my satisfaction. But everybody who has even a cursory 
 knowledge about Buddhism would most certainly agree that "Absolute"
 and "Nirvana" are two different entities atleast by the world of
 Ideas.

One must first learn that Buddha was an agnostic, and even during his
death he did not know if God existed.

I think personalities like Buddha and his teachings should be given
proper treatment before it is publicly announced as such.

Nothing to undermine the editorial board. Just a pointer to "CAUTION".

somdutt
