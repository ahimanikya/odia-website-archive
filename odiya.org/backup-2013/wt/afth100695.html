Dear Amitabh,
Bhabuthiba hatath lekhili kahinki. As usual I tend to remain extremely busy
these days. However, as I was reading your weekend thoughts, I had a feeling of
sharing my ideas on these issues. You can separately post my views in the
ornet.

Most often than not we tend to accept and believe in the status quo, without
ever trying to ask why it was so, or if the things could have been little 
different. As has been succinctly accepted and believed by many, the belief
that the male domination of our society was due to inherent adventuring nature
of man, is in fact a total lie. It tended to be so in organised society like
ours.

But not always. Take the tribal society Kibutzin of Israel. Here by law male
and female enjoy same status. Both take equal responsibility of maintaining and
propagating family. In fact all tribal societies(minus modernisation) tend to
exhibit this behavior. It is only when we organised into society, whereby we
laid down certain rules, thereby denying certain just rights to woman in the
name of religion or deep rooted prejudice and bias, that a society become male
dominated.

One most important thing that we all tend to forget, often most women I have
talked to so far, is that behind this treacherous design, was a subtle fact,
the fact of women bearing the responsibility of bearing her child for 9 months.
The mother nature has so made women that she remains almost incapacitated for
first two to three months and last one to two months of her pregnancy. And 
society capitalized, atleast in organised society, to a greater extent on this,
to effectively remove power from women, in an excuse to protect her progeny.
But one forgets that her progeny is for the general benefit of the society as a
whole, and as such she has been equally participating in social chore, in no
less terms than a man.

The trend is now reversing, with science taking care of the child bearing
process, making it easy.

For a general idea on how an equitable society based on gender could be
achieved among Kibutzin of Israel, please refer to "Sociology" by Horolombous. Refer to
the account by noted American Sociologist Talcott Parson.

For a general behavior of tribals in Indian context refer to Indian Sociology
by Mandelbaum. Refer particularly to accounts on some matriarchal communities.

somdutt
