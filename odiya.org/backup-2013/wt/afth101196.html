Dear Ms.Swarnakar,

Thank you for your comments on the WT article "Indian Family". About your comments: when I read the article, I found it very fascinating and to me,it described a typical oriya joint-family in such vivid detail. My lack of awareness and knowledge of joint-family in other parts of India could be the reason, why I thought so. But I still think, there were lots of description of typical rituals (religious or otherwise) and practices, which are predominantly observed in oriya families; which prompted my remark. But let me say again, it's just may be my inexperience. I never meant to say that, joint families only exist in Orissa or anything like that. The concept is very much Indian and it's practiced all over the country. Although, as you have rightfully pointed out, the pattern has been shaken with change of time.

> Just a quick reflection

After reading, the firs picture which one gets is that it is an indian family portrait and not of just Oriya or Bengali etc. Another point I want to object is at the comment of the sender. Why did you think it was fifties portrait. Even today it continues. Though the pattern of joint family is shaken by the immigration of people for work still one sees that on festivals or during the marriages the whole family units It is still common among the families where the male members continue in the family business to have common property and common kitchen. The security it offers to children and less fortunates is such that it will take time to break it.

Last week we had a visitor from Macmillan, India. During her talk she remarked in South we have a strong tradition of Shakti-Pooja. As natural I could not keep quite and said it is in whole India. In Bengal they worship Durga and Kali, In Gujrat and Rajsthan it is Amba. Most of the North Indian Hindus keep a fast for nine days and worship their family Godess. They combine it with an uninterupted Ramayan reading and join with Rama's killing of Ravana in Dashahara festival. We have a beautiful epic poem Ram Ki Shakti Pooja by NIRALA. The story is based on Rama's desperate mood and his worship of Godess to give him strength to fight the evil. Thus he openly declares the supremecy of mother's power. The lady (though an editor in India )said she had no idea about the Shakti-worship in other parts of India at least she knew about Bengal. So when I saw your comment I felt the culture is one in whole country. Recently in India Today the Polish Ambassdor to India, who is also a well known scholar in Indian culture and literature and a Ph.D. from B.H.U. wrote " There is one thing that units this country and it is the culture. You may be from North South East or west you are tied with this cultural bond which makes us feel one.
