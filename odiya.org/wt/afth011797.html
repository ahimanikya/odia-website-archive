To:     The Editor, Weekend Thought.

Dear Editor,

With great interest, I went through the views of Prof Mohapatra and also the most considerate reactions of Subrat and Manoranjan. I think, all of them are absolutely right in their own respective stands. I cannot but agree with Subrat, when he induces me to look at gender as a biologist. Neither could I differ from Manoranjan, when he reminds me the everyday life of a village girl in a tiny settlement in remote Orissa or elsewhere. Although I do not intend to present a lenghty submission here, I must emphasize that the issue is mostly lies in the "state of mind". The state of mind is conditioned by the societal contingencies, which in turn, is a product of history. Historically, states like Orissa had undergone a great deal of external aggression and political turmoil, leaving the women as the most vulnerable group. The obvious 'paternalistic protection' towards women finally became an indicator of their status vis-a-vis the men. Most unfortunately, we are the victims of this history, and are the unintentional bearers of this legacy of looking at the women as just "helpless". But at the same time, I am optimistic that our women are galloping forward to regain/reclaim their status, which the nomadic "Aryans" had granted to their women about 3500 years ago. My optimism is not unfounded, as I have been a curious witness to the practice of "Shradhha" (the annual offering of pinda to the dead ancestors), when my father recites a "mantra", which literally means that without the cooking of the "pinda" by my mother, he will be ineligible to offer it to his ancestors. In fact, in Hindu practice, a widower does not have the ritual sanction of feeding his ancestors. This is indicative of the complimentarity of women in men's life. It is very much inherent in our thoughts and actions. Only we have to realize it and rise to the occasion. I am sure, we are leading in the right direction (as I see the concern of so many towards women abuse), and the time is not very far, when we see our women emerge strongly enough not to be any more abused by men. In the mean time, I may only remind, LET NOBLE THOUGHT PREVAIL UPON ALL OF US TO LOOK AT OUR OWN WOMEN AT HOME AND WORK PLACE AS NECESSARY DIVINE COMPLIMENTS IN FULFILING OUR NOBLE DUTIES OF CREATING AND RECREATING A WONDERFUL UNIVERSE, which does exist beyond the logistics of jargonic democracy or autocracy.

It is time for us to pause and think if there is any substance to all these. It makes it easier for us to understand it in today's world -where physical strength is no more the criterion in deciding the bread-earner. Women, when given the opportunity and encouragement, have competed successfully with men. Recent studies have talked about the differences between the brains of men and women - something which may make us think that there may be inherent strengths and weaknesses between the genders. Advanced brain scanning techniques have shown that women have stronger connection between the two halves of the brain, making them better at verbal and non-verbal communication. On the other hand the lack of communication between the halves may make men better at concentration and so you may say that men would make better chess players. However thisbe is is a gross oversimplification at best as the variation between the genders pales in comparision with individual differences. World's youngest grand-master is a girl. Judit Polgar attained the title at the age of 15(?). Similarly, Shakespear's intuitive understanding of the human emotion speaks of the versatility of the brain regardless of the gender.

Discrimination is caused due to a sense of superiority. Do you and me have any reason to feel that males are the superior gender?

Sriketan Mahanti
Champaign, IL
