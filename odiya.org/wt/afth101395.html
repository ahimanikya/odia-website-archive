Hello everyone,
 
In the last ornet article Minoti Sahu asked if the US born indians should 
fund their college education by taking loans, working etc. or should they
be funded by the parents. I think they should just like americans earn their
education. That experience not only teaches independence but also in my
knowledge makes them more open minded to various options in life. Such qualities
always form the bottomline for innovations in any field ( technical, medicine 
and you name it). On the otherhand such independence can also lead to lack of 
family ties and all the other social problems seen here. 

I agree with the author that ideal situation would be to hold on to the traditional 
Indian values and inculcate the good American qualities. I feel that it is like 
having the entire cake and eating it too. I think there has to be a compromise 
between the two to some extent.  Again, I may be wrong and am willing to hear about 
this from others. 

The reviewer has tried to draw on his personal experiences in India and commented 
on the article. To some extent I think it is not relevant to the topic at hand. 
It is difficult to compare indian and american culture, like comparing apples and 
oranges. Also, only a very small fraction of the indian population actually obtain 
education through the government scholarships. 

Abinash

The author has made an commendable effort in trying to present
the social customs of supporting one's children both financially
and morally during their education in India.  She also has
pointed out the differences in the custom here, and in the states.
Back home, irrespective of your financial status you support
your children during their education if you want them to be
educated, no matter what the outcome is going to be.  Even
most of us after coming to this country do depend on our
parents for support (may be initially.)  The author has drawn
the attention to the fact that American students try to be
self sufficient, and some may have to go through unpleasant
situations in order to accomplish the self-sufficiency, which
is not a fact in India.

Though not explicitly mentioned in the article, the author
has posed the question to Indian parents living here about
the financial preferences of their children's educational
expenses.  Should a parent go by the child's choice of
school, discipline, etc. and pay for all the expenses,
or force the child to study what the parent wants him/her
to study and then only make a plan to pay for schooling,
or make a firm decision of not paying at all?

The author has skillfully succeeded in providing some 
appetizing food for thought.

Brajendra
