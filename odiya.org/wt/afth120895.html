Subject: Mother! A critical look



The subject of mother and the associated motherhood has been subjected
to undue emotion when it comes to looking at it critically, particularly
in Indian context.

We must distinguish between the mother of an infant who is just born
and must, therefore, be taken care off, and the mother of a full grown
man. They are two different personalities, and hence, must be analysed
accordingly.

Formerly, she is a creator and a preserver. She goes beyond just human
being. She is universal. She is the collaborator with nature, in her
design at creating this wonderful world (or should I say ugly world !).
In this sense she is no way different than any other mothers. She 
belongs to living world. In this sense she is equal with all animal 
kingdom. SHE IS THE ENIGMA OF NATURE !!!!

But in later case she is an ordinary human being, manisfested with
all the ills of being human being. Here she is a selfish mother, a
different personality indeed.

In that sense my mother of my childhood is significantly different from
my mother of now.

I draw this idea from my experience of motherhood in India. It is 
reflected in the all pervading strained relations between sasu-bahu
(mother-in-law<->daughter-in-law), bahu-nanada(daughter-in-law <->
sister-in-law) and such other relations. Also it is reflected in 
many of us talking ill about other mothers while blindly praising
our own mothers. 

Whenever I discover people  talking ill about other mothers, I 
immediately realise the wanton failure of our motherhood. Ofcourse,
I have always sensed the misery, and hence failure, of our motherhood
in other relations as discussed above.

I write this as I have similar bitter experience here in Houston too.

So, I think we are matured enough not to take motherhood blindly.

A true mother should first teach her kids how to respect other mothers,
then own community, then other communities, own language, then other
languages, own religion, then other religions, caste creed, states,
nations ........ and the universe. In the absence of this, as is now,
out motherhood is yet to go long way to reach its perfection.

It is not meant to hurt anybody's feeling.


Somdutt
