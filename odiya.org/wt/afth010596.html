Hi! Debasmita Bhaina,

Hope the year of 1996 started off on the right foot for you. I 
liked your article. It is a very detailed analysis of the 
different forms relationships between the husband and the wife, 
within the institution of marraige.  What I admire is that the 
article has no biased opinions.  But reading this article has 
caused a lot of buried feelings about the institution of marraige 
to resurface. More specifically, the question that comes to my 
mind is, when can you really say that a married couple is truly 
happy and in harmony? I believe that both these words `happy' and 
`harmony' are relative terms.  
 
From experience, and from observing a lot of young and older 
married couples I have come to the conclusion that even where 
there exists a relationship of equality (at least to some degree) 
between the two partys the couple is not necessarily very happy. 
To digress from your article for a minute, it is quite ironic to 
read that even our GODS at one time or another have practised 
polygamy or adultery and yet we humans (Indians) who perhaps 
created god (after all there has got to be some truth to 
Darwin's theory of Evolution) look upon polygamy with such 
disdain and treat adultery with such vengance.  So are only Gods 
allowed to digress from the realm of a happy marraige where 
supposedly the husband and the wife lead a life of perfect 
harmony?

Rest later

Bye.

Shanta

There is a golden rule to a happy relationship:

"May we not find fault with others, 
but try and find our own faults."
 
This has been ascribed to Sarada Devi, Sri Ramkrishna 
Paramahansa's wife, reverred as Holy Mother by the 
Ramakrishnaites. True as it is, very difficult to follow.

I also encourage people to read the three
satakas by Raja Bhratrihari to reflect on 
the pragmatism of life from an astute observer.
You will enjoy it.  We do need translations
of these texts into Oriya.  For the curious,
Raja Bhatrihari was the elder brother of
Raja Vikramaditya and lived in Ujjain.

I think it was a nice analytical article - love your analogies 
with our mythical figures. Very witty. And I really liked the 
very perceptive depiction of a wife's and a husband's different 
stages - ChandramukhI, SuryamukhI and JwALAmukhI. Husmukh, 
Deshmukh and Lajjyamukh.

I have 2 doubts. Please correct me if I am wrong.

1.  In your article you mentioned that SaraswatI is addressed as 
    ChanchalA.

    As far as I know LaxmI is the one who is referred to as 
    'ChanchalA'. Not SaraswatI. LaxmI is the goddess of wealth 
    and riches which is never stable or dependable. That's why 
    LaxmI is referred to as ChanchalA.

    And SaraswatI , the Goddess of knowledge and wisdom is 
    supposed to be very calm and 'Stitadhi'. Knowledge is 
    something you can not loose unless LaxmI(wealth) her sapatnI 
    goes to  your head and takes over. 

    And also LaxmI has been described as Teri (cross eyed - so is 
    wealth) and malina varnA (not fair - wealth and riches have a 
    dark side to those).

2.  And also in your article you said that PArvatI created
    KArtikeya and Ganesha.

    If I am not mistaken - I think PArvatI had no hand in the  
    creation of KArtikeya (the best looking person in the whole 
    universe - or should I say Mr... Universe!)? Shiva solely by 
    himself made KumAr-Sambhava possible. 

    And I have even heard that Ganesha was the expression of 
    PArvatI's competitive spirit as an answer to Shiva's KArtika. 
    Shiva had no hand in the creation of Ganesha.

Am I mistaken? Please let me know.

Thanks for giving us such a nice witty article. 

Rest later.
