Hi,
I was pleasantly surprised to read an article where the Indian males are "stereotyped"...... especially coming from the opposite gender. I do agree with Somdutt that the author has gone a little overboard in his criticism.... but hey, that is not an uncommon situation you see. I have more problem with the reviewer's comments than with the author.....I strongly disagree with the reviewer when he indicated that "women are equally responsible in this design". How so ??? Let's take the same example that he used. A dowry victim still shares the same bed with her husband, have kids, etc. Now, why does she do that ??? i mean, does she have any other alternatives ???? It is definitely debateable. Let's say she opposed and hated her husband, and consequently will not live in that environment (meaning leave him). Where will she go then ??? if she is not working she has to take help of her parents/relatives. Is that really a viable option (espicially low income families, where the parents have spent considerable amount of money for the marriage/dowry) ?? If she is working, i think she stands a better chance... but will not be very well treated by the society (which is full of such men in the workplace). When the alternatives does not look viable/doable/worse the girl accepts her fate and lives her life. And if the girl is smart, confident (self-esteem) and independent she will not agree to go thru a marriage where dowry is involved in the first place.

There are a few of them but not a whole lot because it requires not only education, but the right kind of upbringing and personality to do that. I do agree with Somdutt that behaviors need to be changed... but i will say it requires more than that.... a paradigm change in gender role attitudes as well!!!

Indian males, whether they are the "cream of the crop" or otherwise, when it comes to choosing a partner, look for girls that are "submissive", and so do their families.... they do look for reasonable amount of intelligence in their partners... but when they meet intelligent, smart, and independent girls who are professionals like them, they feel threatened..... thinking they can not hold the rein as the sole head in his family. I mean they can still sing the song (meaning can talk of equality, independence, sharing, etc) but when it comes to dancing to the tune (action) they find they have a lot of problems in their musculoskeletal system. I have not come across very many of such individuals. As for the "liberal open-minded" Indian males, we have a long way to go guys (the definition of liberal open-minded male again, is very subjective).

Ranjita

Hi, Guys:

I guess enough has been said about the sexist attitude of the Indian male. Any stereotyping is bound to invoke a reaction about the outliers & exceptions. But the consensus is that there is indeed a problem. Unless we recognise & accept that there is a problem we will never attempt to solve it. So, on that count I agree with Ranjita's rejoinder.

I would like to go deeper than Ranjita's position that male dominance stems from economic power. I have seen families where the mom, though doesn't work wields substantial control over decisions affecting her & her family. I have also seen families where the wife, though well educated & employed is dominated over. I feel what breaks the tyranny of social conditioning is enlightened parenting. At the same time, accepting that the Indian male has a tendency to be sexist/chauvinistic would be a first step towards correcting a behaviour that is inherently unfair.

I think the burden to correct the situation falls equally heavily on women. A woman has the right to communicate and let the man know when his behavior is unacceptable. How does one achieve it in a traditional arranged marriage setting is yet another issue.

Equality in the relationship of sexes goes far beyond basic rights of women. However, I know one thing is true that wise men & women of all times have realised that equality goes a long way in making a happy family. I have seen a lot of happy couples of my parents' generation and I have seen that almost always it is a relationship based on respect and equality that stems from it - even though their roles in the family follows the classic gender separation.

We could be smart and argue on & on about the issue or we could be wise and do something about it.

Ram

Hi Everybody,

This is in response to the Weekend Thoughts article of 1/19/96 called 'The Mask'.

I think the article had a deplorable and condescending overtone. Men have been categorised as abusive, spineless and been belittled by one of their kind. India has a very old and rich cultural heritage. According to our beliefs, when a woman drops whatever she is engaged in to bring something to her husband because he asked for it does not mean that she is oppressed. Maybe she (Smita) has been taught and has understood true Indian manners. You are supposed to drop everything and do the bidding of the person you love/respect. Am I being too presumptuous in assuming that the wife loves her husband? I have seen my mother do that and that was not because my father is a tyrant.

Traditionally the man of the house went out to work and earn the bread for the family, whereas the woman stayed home, took care of the family and home. But with changing times, and with more women going out to work, all the households I have seen do have division of labor. I have yet to meet a man who, as per the author, goes after his wife criticising her housekeeping. I was under the impression that men and women are inherently different in most aspects of life. Including housekeeping! More women tend to work inside the home, whereas more men tend to do the outside chores. It is up to the couple to decide upon their division of labor and not for anyone else to pass judgment upon that. As long as they are happy, why try to second-guess their happiness.

Maybe most people do not adapt completely to western culture when they leave India and settle in some foreign country, but that is, I would think, because one cannot give up years of heritage and culture and tradition and live a life without an identity. There is everything good in praising your homeland with the loudest of voices.

These are some of my thoughts. One should try to be objective rather than subjective when discussing what could be controversial topics.

Sincerely,

Minoti Sahu
