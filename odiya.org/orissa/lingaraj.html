The Lingaraj Temple

[Unrecovered image: ling.gif]

The great Lingaraj temple soars above the city of Bhubaneswar and represents the pinnacle of Orissan temple architecture. It has, in fact, been described as 'the quintessence of Orissan architecture'.The magnificent Lingaraj temple, 147ft. high from the plinth, was built by Lalatendu Keshari of Somavamsi dynasty in 617-657 A.D. The Natamandap and Bhogamandap were later added to the temple that has evolved over different periods of rule.

The North Indian sikhara style of temple building reached its final form in the culmination of the Lingaraj temple. This is featured by a tower which begins to curve inwards at about one third of its height, with rounded top crowned by a flat stone disc called amalaka and a finial called kalasa. This graceful curving tower moves upward and emphasized by deep vertical inlets, but its firm base on earth is very evident. Like most Orissan temple, the Lingaraj temple is built as a series of four halls. The hall of offerings is called Bhoga Mandir, dancing hall is called Nata Mandir, assembly hall is Jagamohana , and the sanctum sanctorum with its crowning tower (sikhara) is called deul. The deul of the Lingaraj temple reaches a height of just over 180 feet. It is completely curvilinear, and the extraordinary soaring tower can be seen to incorporate miniature replicas of itself, in turrets inserted on the ribs of the spire. The other three elements of the temple, leading one by one to the shrine, are also roofed with characteristic towers of smaller size, carrying the eye to the main sikhara. The Lingaraj temple adds two new structures: the natamandir and the bhogamandir, which are not found in early temples of Orissa during the same period. The former structure was undoubtedly the sign of rising prominence of the devadasi (temple prostitution) system. Many of the sculptures on the temple itself represent increasing range of religious and musical activities carried out at the temple.

One of the noticeable features of the Lingaraj temple is its degrading architectural beauty as one retreat from the deul away towards the bhogomandir. This is so because Lingaraj temple was built over a period of three dynasties. The deul was built during the prospering Kesari dynasty. Under the onslaught of Muslim invasion the later days dynasties had not been doing very well. This is reflected by the seeming austerity of the bhogomandir, which was built by the Gangas.

By the time the Lingaraj temple was built, the Jagannath cult had become widespread throughout Orissa. It is exemplified by the fact that the deity of this temple Svayambhu linga, is not, as in all other cases, strictly a Shiva linga. This is considered to be a 'hari-hara' linga. 'Hari-Hara means half Shiva, half Vishnu.

There are 150 subsidiary shrines wihtin this huge complex, many of them are really interesting in their own right.
