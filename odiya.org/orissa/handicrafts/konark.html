THE SUN TEMPLE AT KONARK (SURYA DEULA)

(Only if the temple is constructed properly, can it fit into the model of the Universe. Conversely, if the measurements are perfect, there will be perfection in the Universe as well.)

Mayamatam (an ancient architectural text)

The Surya Deul or the Konark Temple exemplifies the above posthulation in its true form. Because of this probably once Rabindranath Tagore said of the Konark Sun Temple-"Here the language of man is defeated by the language of stone."

The Konark temple, otherwise known as the "Black Pagoda", built in the 13th century by King Narasimhadeva of Ganga Dynasty, is a temple dedicated to Surya, the sun-god, and was formerly one of the largest and most splendid temples of India, seemingly even larger than Lingaraj Temple of Bhubaneswar. It is one of the most stunning monuments of religious architecture in the world, and is considered by many as the culmination of Orissan temple architecture. The tower (The Sikhara), was over 200 feet high. But this has fallen long since. However the great assembly-hall survives. Unlike the other temples of Bhubaneswar-Konark-Puri region that of Konark had the two smaller outer halls completely separate from the main structure, and assembly-hall and tower were built on an imposing platform, round which were carved meticulously crafted twelve pairs of decorated wheels, each 10 feet in diameter. The entrance is reached by a broad flight of steps, flanked on either side by prancing horses, the whole representing the chariot in which the sun-god rides across the heavens. The court of the temple was decorated with large free-standing sculptures of great strength and beauty.

Surya has been a popular deity in India since the Vedic period and many passages occur in prayer to Him in the Rig Veda, the earliest of sacred religious text. So the image of the sun-god traversing the heavens in his divine chariot, drawn by seven horses, is an ancient one. But the idea of building such an immense temple in the shape of a chariot, however is not an ancient one, and is something that was being conceptualised for the first time. It indeed, was a breathtakingly creative endeavour. Construction of the huge edifice is said to have taken 12 years, with the deft participation of seemingly 1200 astute craftsmen of widely varying faculties.

The main tower had followed the same general form as the towers of the Lingaraj temple. However, its height exceeded the former. The Jagamohana (porch) structure itself exceeded more than 200 feet in height. The wheels surrounding the tower and porch are exquisite, and in themselves provide eloquent testimony of the genius of Orissa's sculptural tradition.

The third major component of the temple complex was the detached Natamandira ( hall of dance), which is in front of the temple. Once 22 subsidiary temples stood within the enclosure, but only two, the Vaishnava Temple and the Mayadevi temple, to the west of the tower have survived. At either side of the main temple one will gasp at the colossal figures of royal elephants and royal horses.

It is still a mystry why this amazing structure was built here. Who provided the architectural design of the whole structure? The writer of " The Chariots of God" has attributed this ability to the visit of some extra-terrestrial beings from outer space, and their imparting of knowledge therein. As per one popular legend, one son of the god Krishna, Samba, seemingly very handsome, once ridiculed a holy, although ugly sage. The sage took his revenge by luring Samba to a pool where Krishna's consorts were bathing. While Samba stared, the sage surreptiously left the place and summoned Krishna to the site. Enraged by his son's impropriety with his stepmothers, Krishna cursed the boy with leprosy. Later however, he realised his mistake. But curse had already flown out of mouth. Samba then performed 12 years penance to Surya on the seashore where the Surya Deula stands now. Pleased with his devotion sun-god relieved him of the curse. In thanksgiving, Samba erected a temple at the spot. However, historical fact remains that Narasimhadeva, the builder of the temple, probably erected the temple as a victory monument, after a successful expedition against Muslim invaders. It is also said that the king's mother was a devotee of the Sun god, and it was her desire to dedicate a temple on His honor. The temple's location was to be so chosen such that the first ray of the sun had to greet the deity in the Garbhagruha.

In any case, the temple that was left behind for us is a chronicle in stone of the social, domestic, military, and religious aspects of the thirteenth century Orissa. Every inch of the temple is covered with sculpture of an unsarpassed beauty and grace, in tableaux and freestanding pieces ranging from the monumental to the miniature. The subject matter is fascinating and widely varied. Thousands of images include deities, celestial and human musicians, dancers, lovers, and myriad scenes of courtly life, ranging from hunts and military battles to the pleasures of courtly relaxation. These are interspersed with birds, animals, mythological creatures, and myriad of intricate botanical and geometrical decorative designs. The jewel-like quality of Orissan art is evident throughout, and its human perspective makes the sculpture extremely accessible.

EROTICA AND SURYA DEULA

Orissan temples, and especially the Sun temple at Konark, are famous for their beautiful and quite explicit erotic imagery. The exceptionally frank eroticism of many of the Konark sculptures has given the "Black Pagoda" a rather infamous reputation.
