Tribals constitute over 23 percent of the entire population of Orissa, the highest percentage in any Indian state, next only to Nagaland. Kandha, Saora, Gond, Santal, Paraja, Gadaba, Koya, Bhumija, Bhuyan and Juang are the some of the prominent tribes among over 60 tribes recognised in the Constitution of India. The following is a description obtained from Tourism Directory, Orissa. 
Tribals of Orissa
-----------------


      Orissa is one of the States in Eastern India which can boast of
  a vast tribal population belonging to diverse races and culture.
  The majority of this population inhabits the forests and hilly
  regions of Mayuibhanj, Kendujhar (Keonjhar), Phulbani, Sundargarh
  Sambaipur, Balangir, Kalahaiidi and Koraput districts.  Orissa
  has as many as 62 tribes, the important of which are-Kondh,
  Koya, Bonda, Gadaba, Paraja, Santal, Juang, Oraon, Saora, Gond, Ho,
  Kharia, Bhuyan, Binjhal, Bathudi, etc.    Each of these tribes is
  marked.for its oWn culture, different from the other.  A glimpse
  of their cultural activities may not be out of place to corroborate the same.


Kondh
-----
     The Kondhs are one of the well known tribes of Orissa who were
famous in history for their Meriah Sacrifice (human sacrifice).  They
are found almost in all the districts of Orissq but mostly
concentrated in large numbers in Phulbani, Sambal- pur, Balangir,
Koraput and Ganjam districts.  

A Kondh's life is full of festivities. The important festivals are at
the time of sowing and harvesting.  During festivals, feast is,
accompanied by singing, dancing and drinking.  At the time of sowing
seeds they worship Dharani Deota (Thanapenu) and make animal sacrifice
(human sacrifice was practised in the past) to ensure- good crop. The
village women sing and dance joining hands and the boys standing
around also sing and the entire atmospheria becomes joyous.  During
"osarupenu" festival tiey resort to worship on the hills and enjoy the
day with feasting and dancing.  Their weapon is bow and arrow by which
they defend themselves from wild animals.

Koyas
-----
     Koyas constitute the principal tribe of Malkangiri in Koraput
district and widely distributed throughout the area.  A motorable
road connects the new Malkangiri subdivision with ieypore town.

     Koya villages are situated on patches of clearings in the
midst of forests.  Approach to village is by a narrow foot path
from the road and some other villages are also by the side
of the road.


     In religious and marriage ceiamoniesp Koyas resort to music
 and dance.  The religious ceremonies of the Koyas are mainly
 four-Bijja pandu, Kodta pandu, Bumud pandu and lkk pandu.
 Bijja pandu is observed in May-June every year and Bumud pandu
 in January-February which are suitable occasions to enjoy their
 colourful dances.  They p6rform ceremonial dances wearing bison
 horns on their heads.


Bondas
------

In the district of'Koraput, on the high hills to the north-west
of the river Machhakundl live the wildest, rudest and possibly the
interesting tribe whith is known as Bonda or'naked people.
scanty dress of BondQ- women and homicidal tendency of
Bonda males have made them most fascinating and thus they stand
in sharp contrast from the neigh bouring castes and tribes of Orissa.
Bondas call themselves 'Ramo' and speak a very difficult
Austro-Asiatic language.

  Amidst picturesque scenes, Bondas live on high hills surrounded
fruit groves.  They are generally agriculturists and are expert
cultivators.  The irrigated and terraced paddy iields on high hills
prove their skill in riep- cultivation.

  Bonda dormitories for the unmarried boys and girls are very
interesting.  They are mainly a short of matrimonial agencies.  The
young boys are allowed to visit the girls' dormitories of different
villages in the night and join with them in fun, music and dance.
Thus they establish intimacy and select their sweet hearts by forcing
bangles into their hands.
  
Bondas tend to enjoy a higher degree of economic sufficiency.

    Gadaba
    ------
    
    The Gadabas are ons of the most colourful and primitive tribes
    of Orissa. They are one of the earlV settlers of this country and
    trace their origin to the time of Ramayana. They are generally
    seen in the adjoining mountain tracts of Andhra, Orissa and Madbya
    Pradesh. Their main concentration is in the district of Koraput and
    their maximum populaton is in Jeypore, Nandapur, Semiliguda,
    Sunabeda and Pottangi areas. They have their own language
    'Gutab' and are not a migratory folk. They live permanently in large
    villages. They are generally agriculturists. During religious cere-
    monies and annual hunting 1hey resort to music and dance. But
    in thoir dormitories for boys and girls, music and dance are regular
    features.
    
    Santal
    ------   
    Santals are mostly found in the districts of Mayurbhanj and
    Balasore.
    
    Their numbers are moro in and around Similipal National
    -Park area. The language of the Santal is one of the oldest tongues
    of India, known as "Santali". The Santal villages have a characteri-
    stic patterm Every village consists of a long street with rows of
    
    dwellings on either side. Their household furnitures are very
    rneagre. Hunting is done with bow and arrow which they love
    very much. Their dress consists of a dhoti for male and saree
    for female They love to use silver ornaments. Their musical
    instrumentS consistS of Madal, Dhumsa, a bamboo Flute and horn
    Trumpet.
    
    Marriages by force and intrusion are very rare. They practice
    Seven forms of marriages.
    
    Thakur or Supreme Being (Sun god) is worshipped by them
    for good crops and all other necesSities. They are agriculturists
    and their economy is based on agriculture.
    
    They generally eat twice a day but are very fond of 'Handia'
    (liquor) prepared from boiled rice. Liquor from Mahul flowers is
    also prepared.
    
    During Makara Festival (13th/14th January) they resort to
    feasting and dancing in large numbers although music and dancing
    accompany their all social functions. Tourists can enjoy their colour-
    ful dances during Makara Festival which is the best time to visit
    the wild life sanctuary at Similipal.
    
    Paraja
    ------    
    Parajas are mostly found in Koraput, Kalahandi and Sundar-
    garh districts. Their language is "Parij" but many of them now
    speak Oriya fluently.
    
    The Parajas have a number of totemistic septs like Bagh
    (Tiger), Bokda (Goat), Netam (Dog), Pendki (Dove) and the like,
    and harming Or eating the totem animal is forbidden. Instead such
    animals should be respected.
    
    Dormitory life does not play an important rale in their social
    set up. They worship various gods and goddesses residing in hills
    or forests. During weddings Parajas resort to dance and music.
    
    Juang
    ------      
    Among the original tribes found in the State of Orissa, the
    Juangs form a major group in the districts of Keonjhar and Dhenka-
    nal. They speak a language very akin to Mundari and known as
    "Kolarian".
    
    Marriage is the most important stage in the life cycle of the
    Juangs. Forcible marriage is prohibited. If both the parties are
    satisfied with each other, the marriage is settled.
    
    The traditional weapOns of the Juangs are the bows and
    arrows, the axe, spear and the battle axe. They love hunting, but not
    so much as the Santals.
    
        
    They have firm belief on the 'Mahaprabhu' (the Almighty
    creator) and Basudha (the Mother Earth) and observe many Hindu
    festivals.
    
    Magha Parba (during January- February corresponding to
    M&gha Purnima) is their most important festival. This daY the
    people prepare varieties of cakes and offer to Bana Devi jn anti-
    cipation of good crOp and in the evening assemble in the 'majang'
    and take part in music and dance which continues for the whole
    night.
    
    It is now clear that different tribes have distinctive cultures of
    their own. It not only varies in their socio-economical life but also
    in religious ceremonies. Thus the pattern of living pursued by the
    Kondhs of Koraput and Kalahandi is comPIetely different from the
    Juangs of Keonjhar and Santals of Mayurbhanj. Although under
    the impact of modern civilisation and Government Developmental
    programmes, things are changing very fast, yet the tribals have
    retained at many places their traditional way of life.
    
    The most important aspect of tribal culture is the music and
    dance. Among certain tribes (Juang and Santal) youngmen cannot
    find brides unless they acquire special skill in dance and music.
    Dance and music are primarily meant to propitiate Gods and God-
    desses. In social functions like marriage and feasting dance is
    their usual hobby. The dance is performed in a central place.of the
    village or in the communal house and attended by almost all the
    tribesmen.
    
    Each tribe has its own pattern of dance and music. The Paraja
    dance exhibits quick speed, graceful movement and artistic skill where
    as the Gadaba and Koya dances are extremely colourful with head-
    dress of bison horns. Similarly the war dance of Kondhs, the
    exciting rhythm of Santals and Oraon dances make us feel their rich
    heritage, artistic skill and emotional qualities.
    
    But under the impact of modernisation and industrialisation
    things are changing fast and it is high time that our rich tribal
    heritage should be preserved.
