Sudramuni Sarala Dasa, a poet of the latter half of 15th century was a contemporary of Surya king Kapilendradev. He is considered as the originator of written Oriya. He composed Vilanka Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and the Chandi Purana, in that order. Mahabharata, his magnum opus, contains roughly more than one hundred thousand verses, each consisting of two lines and covering 18 volumes of printed page.

Many scholars now believe that Madala Panji, the chornicle of the Jagannath Temple, owing its name to its drum (madala) shape when tied at both ends, was started after Sarala Das's time.

Oriya Bhagavata

"Amrita vinaya vachana
Kahi toshiba pranimana.
 

Dhana arjane dharma kari
Dharme prapata Narahari.
 

Martya mandale deha bahi
Devata hoile marayee.
 

Sakala dehe narayana 
Basanti anadi karana.
 

Karma tohara nija guru
Uddhava kete tu pacharu.
 

Apana haste jihwa chhedi
Ke taara achhi prativadi.
 

Atma kusale sarvasiddhi
Tarai samsara baridhi.
 

Mancharu padi mamu mala
Mote ta daiba rakhila.
 

Sarpare jata kalu mote
Swabhava Chhadibi kemante.
 

Jala jesane sunirmala
Swabhave snigdha sumadhura.
Madhura suruchi bachane
Harasa Kari Sarvajane."

All the above lyrical aphorisms and couplets so familiar to Oriyas are by "Atibadi" Jagannatha Dasa who lived (so I read) from 1487-1547 A.D. -- a contemporary of Prataprudradev and Sri Chaitanya. His magnum opus, "Bhagavata," is a national treasure of Orissa. Bhagavata, based on Srimadbhagavata Purana, is dear to most Oriya families for its timeless philosophical ideas, literary simplicity and lilting lyricism. Bhagavata, as the single-most powerful influence on the values & beliefs of most Oriya people, is enshrined in a "Bhagavata ghara" or "Bhagavata Tungi" in almost every village in Orissa. The linguistic and literary influence and aura of Bhagavata may have saved the Oriya language (and hence, Orissa as a distinct culture-state). A Hindu imagery comes to my mind: if "Shudramuni" Sarala Das's Mahabharata were Brahma, the creator, of Oriya language, then Bhagavata is Bishnu, the language's protector.

I am distressed to say that (as Jnana Pitha winning poet Sitakanta Mohapatra has stated) the Oriyas seem to be characteristically indifferent to history. We did not keep record of the times of the great architectural creations like Rajarani & Konark temples nor of their master-builders. Likewise, we have to be content with the abstruse inferences by most experts who place the time of Bhagavata, the masterpiece of Oriya verses, at a little before the turn of the 16th century.

Experts think that Jagannatha Dasa completed 11 volumes ("skandhas") of the Oriya Bhagavata while the 12th & 13th were added later on by less-gifted poets. Jagannatha's Oriya Bhagavata has 329 chapters and 27158 "pathas" (a patha is 2 lines each of 9 letters), while the epic Sanskrit counterpart has 322 chapters and 18 thousand pathas. A lot of original matter as well as elaborations and explications were added by Jagannatha.
