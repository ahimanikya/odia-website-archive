Perpetually washed by the blue waters of Bay of Bengal, Orissa lies on India's east coast with 482 Kms. of sea-beach. With the alluvial belt running along the sea coast the State consists of rugged tracts dotted with jungle' clad blue hills of Eastern Ghats and occasionally broken by ravines and deep valleys. The valleys with crags and peaks of wild beauty over. hanging them rush into the fertile plains, green with crops and .watered by hundred and thousand mountain streams. The villages nestling through the palm, coconut and mango groves with picturesque environment provide excellent atmosphere and repose for the people who seek retreat from the din and bustle of urban life.

Physiography

The State of Orissa is located in the east coast of India in between 17o-48' and , 22o-34' north latitude and 81o-24" and 87o-29' east longitude. The State is bounded' in the north by Bihar, in the west by Madhya Pradesh, in the north-east by West Bengal and in the south by Andhra Pradesh. At the southern point the State recedes from the coast in a widening angle yielding long stretch of coastal belt to its southern neighbour.

The state consists of extensive plateau in the interior with a foreground of wide coastal plain. The surface of the plateau slopes down gently towards Bay of Bengal. The coastal lines run 482 Kms. and show the signs of Progradation -by the develop. ment of lagoons and spits.

Broadly the territory of the State May be divided into four distinct regions. Each region has got a distinctive characteristic of its own.

(1) The Eastern plateau comprises of the districts Of Mayur- bhanj, Keonjhar, Sundargarh and the Palalahara Subdivision of Dhenkanal district. The plateau is an undulating upland frequently intersected by hill ranges and generally slopes down from north to south. The plateau is continuous to the Ohotnagpur plateau and has the hill ranges like Malyagiri (3,896 ft.) in Palalahara Subdivision of Dhenkanal district, Mankernacha (3,664 ft.) in Keonjhar district and Meghasini in Mayurbhanj district which are standing in all their magnificence. The general elevation of the plateau is 3,000 ft,

(2) The Central River Basin comprises of the districts. of Balangir, Sambalpur and Dhenkanal. The region is washed by the important rivers of the State and contains some fertile parts of the State. Isolated hills rising abruptly from the plain adds to the grandeur of the region. Besides the river valleys of the Mahanadi and Tel, there are three other river valleys running parallel to each other in central portion of the State, These valleys are populated and agriculturally prosperous.

(3) The Eastern Hill Region lies to the south and south-west of the Central River Basin stretching for about 248 Kms. in. south- west and northeast. This region comprises of the districts of Phulbani, Ganjam and Koraput. They comprise of wide open upland plateau fringed by forests and hills which have the highest peak in the State. The most important peaks are (a) Deomali (5,486 ft.), (b) Tunia Konda (5,244 ft.), (c) Mayamalli hill (4,921ift.) and (d) Polamakani porbat (5,201 ft.). The average elevation of the plateau is 3,000 ft. and the slopes have valuable forest growth.

(4) The Coastal Belt comprises of the distriCtS of Balasore, Cuttack, Puri and a portion of Ganjam. This region has extensive alluvial tract in between the hills of the west and the salty tracts of the east. The area is generally sandy in Balasore and Puri districts and is swampy and marshy in Cuttack district, Behind the sandy tract lies extensive fertile fields watered by many rivers and canals.

The State is drained by three important rivers, namely, the Mahanadi, the Brahmani and the Vaitarani which flow in south. easternly direction before they meet the sea. The rich alluvial beds of these rivers on two sides have t innumerable villages with extensive ricp fields. Being predominantly rural in character, the State having charming and gay sea resorts in the midst of paini fringed seaboard and vast expanse of back waters like Chilika offers excellent opportunity for a calm and quiet holiday.

The richest mineral belts lie to the western part of the State and they are being,increasingly exploited Near Sambaipur'is India's largest dam on the river Mahanadi and about 201 Kms. away at Rourkela is the country's biggest Steel Town. The State's biggest part, is Paradeep.; only 94 Kms. away from Cuttack, serves as the gateway for the largest concentration of the mineral deposits of the Western Orissa. These are "new temples" which symbolise the people's earnest endeavour for the stirring of a new life and the d-awn of a new epoch.

Thus with deep wood lands, natural springs and wild riverilne gorges, Orissa can boast of varied and picturesque scenic grandeur which a visitor will very much like to contemplate in leisure.

Population

Compared with-foreign countries, 0'rissa is more or less equal in population to Canada and Yugoslovia. In area Orissa may be compared with England and Wales which together are slightly smaller than the area of the State which is 155,70'7-00 square Kms. The average density of population in Orissa is 1,09 persons per square Km. against 221, for India. The coastal region comprising 23-3 per cent of total area of the State has 47-33 per cent of the total population whereas the inland region haying 76-7 per cent of the total area contains 52-67 per cent of the total population. The population as recorded in the Census of 1981, Orissa has got a population of 26,370,271 out of which 13,309,786 males and 13,060,485 females.

The State has 5,915,067, i.e., 22-43 per cent of the total population as tribals. In other words one out of every four persons in Orissa is a tribal. In the hilly tracts of the Western Orissa, there is heavy concentration of tribal population and in the coastal districts they constitute a low percentage.

Orissa has the concentration of India's earliest civilisation which still remains secluded in its pristine form. Their choreographic tradition, their love of song and dance, their customs and institutions are a fascinating subject Of study. A rich store of experiences awaits a tourist if he Prefers to Pay a visit to the tribal settlements where they still live, dance, sing and earn their livelihood without being slightly affected by urban culture. The concentration of the tirbes is found in the districts of- Koreputo Mayurbheni, Sundargarh and Ganiam. Each tribe has a distinct language and culture of its own and their socioeconomic pattern of living varies from tribe to tribe and region to region. Their economic life centres round Pursuits from hunting and food gathering to shifting and settled cultivation.

Through past centuries, Orissa is a meeting place of various cultural waves and various migrations from different parts of the country. With Pronounced rural atmosphere, the people of Orissa prefer to live in their Picturesque villages in the midst of thick vegetation. They like to - live in harmony with their picturesque environments as grace rhythm is visible in everything what they do. They decorate their cottages with lovely murals in white whose motifs are derived from the life they see around. The economic life of the people of Orissa cent res mainly round cultivation and the State is on the threshhold of an industrial revolution. The people are hospitable in nature and Hindi and Bengali are understood and some times spoken by the people, besides Oriya. English is generally understood by literate people of the villages. A spectacle in rural Orissa rewarding to the eyes of a visitor is the Oriya farmer driving his bullock-cart along a palm fringed road in the lovely lush green settings of the countryside. It was the earliest mode of conveyance which never gets,stranded on the road, the sight of which usually abides in the visitor4s memory.

Climate

The entire territory lies in the Tropical zone and hence is subject to high temperature. But the sea exercises a moderating influence on the climate of,the State. The coastal region has got a temperate climate whereas the western region comprising of hill tracts has extreme climate. The maximum temperature is 32.8oC which rises to 38.3oC in April and May. The minimum temperature is 22.8oC which falls to 15.OoC in December. May is the hottest month and December is the coldest month in the State.
