Kartika is the most auspicious month in the Oriya calendar and the Kartika Purnami is observed as the most auspicious day of the year. Legend has it that SriKrishna used to play Raas with the gopis(devotees) on this day. People believe that the deity of Jagannatha contains the remains of SriKrishna and the Lord continues to play Raas with the devotees on this day. The Raas festival in Puri was put in its kirtan and leela form by Chaitanya Dev and his disciples. The Oriya Panchasakha joined him in furthering the festival. Bhagavat is recited all around and Lord's name is chanted with thousand expressions. People have been known to be ecstatic in devotion during the month of Kartika. (More stories/legends exist).

Bali Jatra is observed at Cuttack. Cuttack was a major trading center and for centuries, oriyas would wait for the monsoon to end (no wind from the sea) and the hurricane season to pass by. They would load their boats with prize goods(spices, silk and jewelry) and set sail for the distant islands of Java, Sumatra and Bali. It would take two to three months to reach and they would stay in the far away land for several years doing trade. Then they would return to reach Kalinga by the Visuva Sankranti day (when is it?) to escape the onset of the rains. The reminescence of Oriya names and culture continues to exist in the far islands. The temple architecture there is similar to that in Orissa. A lot of research remains to be done on the Indonesian dialects and the Oriya language. All of us should think about it!

My nostalgia of the day stems from my growing up at Cuttack and going to Mahanadi early in the morning to set sail lamp-lighted banana-stem boats. I loved it! The river would make a splendid spectacle with thousands of light and we would say Aa Kaa Maa Bhai (invocation to ocean Bhairavi to save the boats!). We would come home and go back in the evening to Bali Jatra. It has the flavor of the trade festival as it used to be centuries ago. The food was the greatest. Oriyas are known for good food, but you must eat at Bali Jatra for the finest spicy food that Oriyas prepare. It is different from the Temple food at Puri. The Bali Jatra cuisine moved with the sailors and is coming back to us now as thai and vietnamese food. No meat or fish is cooked on the Bali Jatra grounds.

When I grew older, I loved Bali Jatra and thought a lot about it. I and my friends tried to imagine what kind of cultural celebrations the sailors may be enjoying. Would it be dance, songs or folksy? We learned that the Balinese do the open-air jatra musicals as we do our geetinatya. Thinking perhaps that the geetinatya was the prevailing mode of cultural expression in the sailing days, we started organizing geetinatyas at the Balijatra grounds.

Bijoy Misra
