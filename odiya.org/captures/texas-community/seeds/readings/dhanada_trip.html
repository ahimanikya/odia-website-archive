Introduction : 
============

My trip to Kalahandi and the meetings  with  various  people
related  to  our K/B effort was a much anticipated event for
me. I have been looking forward  to  this  opportunity  ever
since  we  started talking about this issue. For a long time
my fear always has been that a lot of our effort at this end
might  be  totally  mis-guided  without  proper  first  hand
knowledge of the ground realities and contact with the  peo-
ple  we  are trying to help. Thus with much anticipation and
many questions I went home this last Christmas.

In India it is always a great experience to come across peo-
ple  who are punctual, sincere and prompt in their dealings.
>From the beginning to end of my dealings with ABVKA I  found	###
that to be the hall mark of this group that we have selected
for our work. In this report I will first try to give a gen-
eral  account  of  my  travels  followed  by my observations
regarding how I look at the project and the  work  ABVKA  is
doing.

We must realise at the outset that we  are  yet  to  achieve
anything  on  the  ground.  Keeping that in mind I knew that
what I am  going  to  see  is  not  what  our  efforts  have
achieved,  but what is the potential in the future. This was
to be judged by ABVKA's accomplishments  from  the  past  in
surrounding  areas as well as its plans and initial steps in
preparation of their  work  in  Kalahandi  and  to  be  more
specific in the Lanjigarh district.

The general feed back I got in Bhubaneswar as a  prelude  to
my  trip was a overwhelming sense of scepticism among almost	##
everyone that I knew with whom I discussed our intentions. I
kind of expected that. With the mushrooming growth of volun-
tary organizations, talks about Swiss bank accounts  of  one
Association  of  Professional  Social Workers based in BBSR,
social work as a viable carreer and  so  on,  many  educated
friends  including  my  relatives  are  very  cynical of any
effort at developmental work. It seems no one is prepared to
believe  that  it  is  possible  to make an honest effort to
change anything under the current atmosphere with  all  per-
vading corruption in all walks of life.

Rindu Majhi Memorial at Orladani :
================================

Around 3 p.m. we left the Kanyashram and headed to  a  place

this battle and many of them were killed. There  is  a  com-
memorative stone here that describes this event.

along side the jeep. As soon as the jeep over took  him,  he
stopped  and  started  crying  very  loudly. May be there is
nothing unusual about this, but it did strike me at a sensi-
tive spot.

We reached Biswanathpur at mid afternoon. There was  an  EYE
camp  going  on  with much fan fare. Had no time to stop. We
realized that the meeting at Biswanathpur was still  not  on
yet,  so  we  decided to proceed to Lanjigarh. The road from
Biswanathpur to Lanjigarh was the worst yet. The  driver  of
our  jeep  had already complained about his arms being tired
trying to keep the steering  straight.  Anyhow,  we  managed
some how to get to Lanjigarh.

Lanjigarh : 
=========

There are many voluntary organizations and missionaries that
are  working  in Kalahandi area. Although I would have loved
to, I didn't have a chance to meet any  one.  However  every
once  in  a  while  we  would pass some construction or some
prperty that belonged to one or the other voluntary  organi-
zation.  I saw some distribution of sacks of flour with CARE ####
- USA marks on them at Biswanathpur. I was told that  people ###
hardly ate the stuff, but instead fed their animals with it. #What's it mean?

At Lanjigarh we met Mr. Santosh Singh and Mr. Subash Agrawal
who  were  both local buisinessmen and sympathisers of VKA's
work. I found these young people very honest and  knowledge-
able  about  the area's problems. They were bitter about the
governmental negelect and lack of resources  to  create  any
change.  All  of  us  discussed  how  VKA's work is going to
create awareness among people to help themselves and improve
the situation. We were offered tea here.

On our way back to Biswanathpur we saw two high  schools  at
the  edge  of Lanjigarh. Volley ball games were on with many
eager players. We also met the  third  coordinator  selected
for our project Mr. Mohan Munda.

Meeting at Biswanthpur : 
======================

Power cuts are a way of life in most parts of India. When  I
was  home  even  Bhubaneswar had a half hour power cut every
evening. When I visited a village called Dalabad just a  few
kms  away  from  BBSR, I observed that the villagers have to
take 2 hours of power cut. Later I came to realize that  the
VIP  areas  of  BBSR  never  had any power cuts and the more
interior you go, the longer were the cuts.  The  total  con-
sumption  of  power in any typical village in India is prob-
ably less that what is consumed in one of the palatial homes
in a big city. But still it is the villagers who are already
at such big disadvantages that must suffer more! Recently  I
read in Sambad that the power situation in Orissa is under a
crisis and the duration  of  power  cuts  have  become  much
longer. It is so ironic that in the land of Gandhi and Gopa-
bandhu, the privileged should be pampered at the cost of the
poor!

Anyhow, there was no power in Biswanathpur by  the  time  we
came  back.  However we found the village elders namely, the
local Mutha (or Gunthia), the sarapanch and the secretary of
the  Panchyat  Samiti  etc.  were all there at the Panchayat
offfice. We had a meeting in the dark. I was given  the  map
of  the kalahandi that was hanging on the wall. We discussed
the various concerns of the  people  and  the  village.  The
sarapnach  a  young  smart  looking  man  told  us about the
Jawahar  Rojgar  Yojna  (JRY)  under  which  the   Panchayat
received  about  8 lacks ruppees and had spent about 5 lacks #Too little money
by that time. There were  about  30-40  villages  under  the #the real problem?
Biswanthpur  Gram Panchyat and this money was very small for ####
doing anything meaningful and long term.

After this meeting we returned to Narla  to  catch  the  bus
back to BBSR around 9 pm in the night.
