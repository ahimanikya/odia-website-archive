Recently I have been holding conversations with Ms. S. Patnaik who is
energetically interested in a possible SEEDS project for women. Below
are the EXCERPTS to get you interested in the process and substance
of the conversation which I hope will lead to a successful implementaion
of a suitable project.  Please provide your input to any SEEDS member
for consideration during discussion for approval.  --Darshan

>>>From Ms. S. Patnaik:
Few questions and ideas !
I have few women in mind, who I think are sincere and
hopefully will agree to get involved in the projects that
you have in mind, but I do not know anyone, who is
already part of such groups that you have mentioned.
I will keep on asking my friends, if they know anyone.
Actually, how exactly you think we can help  women ?
Can we help women to start a small scale business of
their own or to start a job atleast ? Is it possible ?
I want to know how we can help them. I know some,
who could not get married, because of their health reasons
and some, who can not get out of their rough marraige,
because they can not survive without husband's earning.
These women have Bachelor's and Master's degree in hand
and can not find a job. This is one problem. Look at the
other end, families with working women are falling apart.
Specially in Orissa. Do you know why ? Because we do not
have facilities for day care for babies, women are mostly
depending on their parents or servants. Parents are too
weak to take care of the babies the whole day. Now a days,
servants are not dependable. I have heard horrible stories.
That is why, working women are going through tough time.
If at all,we could introduce the idea of day care, then that
will provide some women employment, some kids better care
and healthy environment to grow and some families a better 
time to make use of. What do you think ?

>>>Darshan's reply:

You have a good idea. But, let's ask these questions:

1) Is the project self-sustainable, say, after 2/3 years?
2) Will it do good to a group not just one or two individual ?
   SEEDS as a group is philosophically oriented to avoid pure
   conventional charity, but it can provide "seed" money and support
   for development of a unprivileged group.  You cited very deserving
   cases, that should be considered by individuals like us, and other
   "charitable" organizations.  But, we can perhaps mold it to the
   idea of sustainability. 

   Can someone lend us their room/house for day-care, and we can
   provide catalytic funds for the equipments/books for a day-care
   suitable to the poor people in Orissa who will be sending their
   kids?  We make sure the employment is fair and without favoritism.
   Can the day-care teachers produce handicrafts or other goods that
   can make them self-reliant in mid-term?

3) We take projects where the beneficiaries contribute nearly half
   of the "cost" in terms of labor and/or small financial
   contributions.  This way everyone has a stake in the project's 
   success.
4) Can we find very reliable people who will oversee the project
   locally? Do you know people there who can send us a proposal
   with details?
5) Can we make the project attractive enough to the beneficiaries who
   will work for it, while making it clear there are no hand-outs or
   wind-falls involved as we are often sharing our very meagre resources ?

If we have satisfied with the answers to above, we can see how much funds
SEEDS can generate.  At present, money-wise SEEDS is very tiny. But
over time, if with the help of people like yourself SEEDS can do good
work/projects, perhaps we can attract donations from many wealthy
people in the community. Perhaps we can write proposals to UN's Sustainable
Development division for funding, and by raising money locally.
