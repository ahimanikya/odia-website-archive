Introduction

 

Sri Vidya Trust (SVT) is a registered voluntary secular organisation functioning in rural areas near Anakapalle in Vishakhapatnam District, Andhra Pradesh. Its goal is to solve poverty-related problems in the region by providing basic education for children, building low-cost permanent houses for the poor, and by organising rural thrift co-operatives. SVT also aims to use communications media to develop skills in adults, and to promote awareness in the community about family issues such as health care. SVT was founded by Dr. Nishtala Prahlada Sastry.

In 1981, Dr. Sastry, then a nuclear scientist at the Tata Institute for Fundamental Research, responding to the calling of his heart, decided to quit his lucrative job and return to live in his native rural area. Using his savings, he purchased a tract of land near Anakapalle, AP, to live a simple and fulfilling life. While he immediately started to feel his personal growth, he also felt that he could contribute positively to improving the condition of the deprived and exploited village residents. His initial efforts were met with stiff resistance from the powerful landlords but his persistence, sincerity, and integrity slowly changed this attitude to the extent that the landlords even started contributing towards the cause. Thus SVT was formed. Dr. Sastry continues to be the motivating force behind the small but dynamic volunteer group which now works tirelessly to bring about positive change in an integrated manner.

Dr. Sastry, the founder of SVT, now works as a voluntary consultant for SVT. Smt. K. Rama, a civil engineer, is the chairperson of SVT now, while Shri. K. V. Prabhakar is the Secretary and Shri. Sitarama Sastry is the Treasurer. Shri. M. K. Gurunath, a civil engineer who has designed and built the Express Towers at Nariman Point in Bombay, works as a voluntary consulting civil engineer for SVT.

Sri Vidya Trust was instrumental in bringing together a large number of villagers to establish a flourishing co-operative cashew plantation. SVT has established and is currently running forty-five schools imparting non-formal education (NFE) in the Sabbavaram Mandal of Vishakhapatnam District. These schools help not only in educating more than 1100 rural children (children who cannot otherwise go to regular schools since they work during the day), but also in establishing a sustainable interest in literacy and self-dependence. SVT has also set up a residential school and appropriate technology training center which combines education with self-employment and income-generation skills. After extensive exploration, SVT has identified and developed a viable design for low-cost dome-shaped permanent housing units that can survive the annual cyclonic activity in the area. Twelve such houses have been built by SVT, with areas ranging from 300 sqft. to 540 sqft., costing Rs. 15,000 each, on average.

This report describes in detail the housing problem, our proposed solution to it, and the implementation issues involved. In Section , we review the current housing situation in India, and the consequent need to construct low-cost permanent structures using locally available materials. In Section , we describe the dome-shaped house that SVT has designed, its cost and size, and its advantages over other possible designs.
