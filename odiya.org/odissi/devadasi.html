The Devadasi Tradition

The tradition of the mahari or devadasi in the temples of India, was one of dancer and sexual initiator. It is a tradition found in relatively recent times throughout the East, traditions as old as those in Ancient Greece and Egypt. The Devadasi was often trained in the art of dance from childhood and she would be officially married to the Temple God at the onset of puberty. The dance form was equally erotic and spiritual which is difficult for the western mind to comprehend. The Devadasi would perform her symbolic lovemaking with the God of the Temple in the dances, which were performed alone for the God in the inner sanctum of the temple or in public. She was also allowed to take lovers and to initiate young men in the sexual arts.

The Temples in India were centres of religious worship, but also of the arts, in the form of theatre, music, poetry, dance, craftsmanship and sculpture, in addition to being centres of philosophy and culture. The Devadasis were not slaves as they are often portrayed but women of high social status, with accomplishment in all of the arts. However because the Western mind could only comprehend the woman as being married or celibate, the devadasi was seen as a prostitute by the British authorities. This cultural gap, along with corruption among some temple priests who forced the devadasis into prostitution, led to the tradition being repressed and almost lost. The dancers of recent years who have put these dance forms back on the map of culture in India were often men, as it was much more difficult for a woman not to be branded with the image of the prostitute.
