One of the qualities of a leader is to persuade his/her team members or co-workers. How do we do that? A few common means for being effective in your persuasion are: 1. Be patient. People need time to understand. Everyone will not jump in instantaneously. 2. Guilt Control. Never feel that you have done something wrong. People react differently to your persuasion. 3. Be polite. Even if someone is trying to abuse your statement or call, please remain calm. Reply after careful thoughts. Do not try to offend anyone. But be objective in your reply. 4. Be consistent. Do not try to agree with everything or everyone. Just stick to your principles. People will respect that. 5. Be persistent. Follow up your request or call with everyone who have not responded. Try to repeat this at least three times keeping in mind your deadline for getting the job done. Drop people out if they do not respond after three calls. They are not team players. 6. Be jovial. Anytime you want to persuade any person, do it with a smile on your face. A smile will make your efforts a lot easier. It will also increase your face value. 7. Stick to time. This is most important both in persisting and also in meeting with your colleagues. 8. Edify. This always helps. Emphasize and subtly express the strength of your team member. For example, I trust nobody else can write this introduction better than you. Could I seek your assistance? 9. Personal Touch. Even when people are busy on individual level, just stop by and ask if you could be of any help. This process gains respect and team spirit. Have an open door policy for sometime. Offer your assistance when needed. 10. Clarity. Always be very clear in your expression and language. This helps to contain undue misunderstandings. Pass on handouts of information that you want your team to read and understand. Specify your expectations very clearly. Also specify their role. The above points are my own thoughts. If you know anymore that you would like to add please feel free to do so. The list is in no way exclusive. Once we have other comments, we will compile all and review once again. Dear Asha: Thanks a lot for posting your own experience. <b>Leadership</b> and <b>Persuasion</b> do not neccessarily go hand-on-hand. A great leader may not be a gread persuador. For example, Mohandas K. Gandhi. He was a great leader but he even could not persuade his own team and created a division of India. On the other hand, a hostage negotiator is not a great leader but an excellent persuador. There are four types of people in a team (unless your team is less than 4 person large <img src=icon_smile.gif border=0 align=middle>). <li>Some are "Outstanders", mostly either silent but dilligent or moderately argumentative but they are either with or ahead of you. These people may also be your competitors. Keep an eye on them. But no need to persuade them. Befriending them and treating them as a strategic partner is sometimes the best approach in a team environment.</li> <li>Some are "Early Adaptors", extremely argumentative, need to be convinced, not difficult to handle, but these are the ones that are with you mostly. They do not compete with you but are loyal followers. Very trustworthy. Recognize them in your team and keep them on your side. Because they are the ones who will convince others that you are the best leader they have seen after Abraham Lincoln.</li> <li>Some are "Late Adaptors", normally silent type but interested, would be listening and taking notes dilligently, would not ask any question, would not reply to any of your questions immediately. They do not see you as their leader. Their leader is one of the early adaptors. You don't make any sense to this person. But they are not disloyal. They wait for their leader to convince them to join the effort of the team.</li> <li>Some are "Laggards", normally disinterested type, these people are not team players at all. Drop them immediately from your team. They are not neccessarily disloyal because they might be interested in some other team. But they will slow your team down if you carry them along. These type of people are normally late by a few minutes to the meeting, would be sitting with a blank stare or drawing your face on their notepad. They will never reply to your question or would start with "could you please reapeat...?".</li> I have worked with laggards and brought them into an active team environment. But it takes a lot of persuasion and effort. I personally do not recommend keeping them in a team. But if you are stuck with them on the top of mount Everest (a very unlikely situation) then you need to work with them.
