Dear readers and writers,

Welcome to the 7th issue of Duradrusti. It is hard to imagine that with the efforts of a handful of busy people could sustain it for seven excellent issues. In some of the previous issues, when I had the opportunity to act as the Editors, I wrote the Editorials in Oriya. This time is again my privilege to write something, although my contribution for in terms of written article is zero. The credits rather go to the entire editorial board and a few other volunteers who do not want their names in print. For this issue, I am acting as an agent to fill the gap, just for the sake of it.

The explosion of knowledge happens in every age, but only a few products of such explosion survive the scrutiny of time. Therefore, before writing this Editorial, I tried to learn from the model Editors and columnists, who write almost daily in the newspapers and/or journals. I respect such people as I feel uncomfortable of writing even one for unforeseeable reasons. Then entered the dilemma of whether to write it in Oriya or English? Like most others, I do not read the Editorials in every journal or newspaper I happen to browse; and assume that the contents of the entire issue are always summarized in the first page or two. If that is true, then what is the job of the Editor other than getting his name in print while the writers, reviewers, proof readers, and layout designers do the hard work?

There is no such definition on the qualifications based on which an Editor can be called ‘poor’ or ‘excellent’. If we look into the newspaper business in Orissa, we find that very few of the ‘Editors’ do the actual writing, and the rest are involved in either active-politics or socializing-politics. Duradrusti is somewhat different in this regard. Here, the aim of the publication of articles for the purpose of promoting an organization (say Myodisa) or carry the beacon of error-free Oriya language. Instead, it is rather a soft-core approach to foster friendship, encourage new writers to polish their talents, and share their views in their prose or poetries.

For any professional journal to survive there must be one or more qualified persons in the Editorial board to correct the mistakes. It is probably right to say that the most deplorable things in the world are the expression of excellent pieces of wisdom in poorly worded languages. But, how can we find them for a journal like Duradrusti, which lives only by the voluntary efforts of its readers and writers. None of the members in this group are in the professions of teaching language or literature. Until then we can have an acceptable solution by separating language/grammar from expression. Expressive literature can be corrected within the limits of available understanding by the current members. At a later date, the quality can be improved with the stepping-in by experts. Among our group we have people who possess excellent knowledge of both the Oriya and English languages. Unfortunately, many of them are unable to participate or even make decisions to participate in the writing or editing processes. Let us hope things will change in the near future and our friends will understand that all those who spare their times do not do so in vain. There are many scholars in Orissa who can take care of the grammars and other tools to enhance the language, but expressing ourselves in a prose or poetry must be done on an individual basis. No real scholar would write for personal gains and many write for fun.

In the last twenty years I tried my best to learn some grammar but have failed miserably. I have a book on the philosophy of the grammarians that discusses the literature of ancient India on the basis of the works of more than 70 of these philosophers of language, ranging from Panini to the modern scholars like Ramajnya Pandeya. However, the material seems to be of limited discussion for common people like me. I had similar trouble even understanding the contents of a small book on Oriya grammar, ‘oDiA bhASAtatwa: bichAra o bishLeSaNa’ by Dr. Dhaneswar Mohapatra. This personal deficiency with my own mother tongue or professional language will never prevent me to accept the truth and continue expressing my ideas in a journal like Duradrusti.

It is my sincere hope that many of you would like to contribute articles for the future issues without the reservation that you may not produce good prose, poetry, or drama for the first time. Those who never dare for crossing the barrier of self-inhibition would never contribute anything for the benefit of others. Until we try, we can never realize our potentials in one arena or another.

As done in the previous issues, we have many interesting articles for your pleasure. I sincerely thank the volunteers who tried their best in collecting, typing, and editing the articles. There will be many errors which must have escaped our attention. This is an electronic edition and with your help we can enter the corrections at any time.

With sincere regards,

Markandeswar Panda

Acting Editor.

January 13, 2007
